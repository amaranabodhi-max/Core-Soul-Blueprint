## Plant card (reference)
LINGERLACE, Odorina tenax; folk names: the Keepsake, Cradle-Lace, the Held Breath. Grows in the moss of old stone thresholds where children once sat waiting at the door, and only opens in fog. Its lace petals stir as if someone near were breathing on them. Gift: keeps the scent of whoever last breathed on it, unfaded, forever. Poison: the one who wears it smells only what it keeps and knows nothing that has grown since; the one who seals it loses her own sense of smell.

**The order:** a noble mother (client by role, no name or title) sends her son to be raised in another noble house, as was the custom, and wants to keep his scent as it is.
- What she made: Madame breathes out over the flower on a stone threshold; the boy breathes into the open locket and Madame closes the lid. The Cradle Lace. Named only, never a recipe; nothing in it is ever described.
- How it ended: twenty years later her son, a grown man, stands in the rain at her door; she looks through the gap and does not open. The price was Madame's: she breathes in her own pomander and smells nothing.
- Now: a client asks "Doesn't it smell like roses?" and she says nothing; at home a child's palm appears on the fogged window and melts. Whose hand it is stays an open thread.
- Takes: her sense of smell.
- Truth of the period (hook; verify before publishing): across the old world, the well-off carried pomanders, pierced metal balls of scent, believing that bad air carried sickness. No contents, no recipe; never tied to a country or city. Her own pomander is part of her look (CANON).
- The clinic jars in 5A are the rhyme: scent sealed in silver then, in jars now. No judgement of the procedures.
