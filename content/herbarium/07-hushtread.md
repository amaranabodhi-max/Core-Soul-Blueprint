## Plant card (reference)
HUSHTREAD, Vestigium tacitum; folk names: the Snow-Walker, Runaway's Sole, the Unwritten Path. Creeps along the edges of moors and under old stone walls, and flowers only under fresh snow. Snow around it never keeps a footprint. Gift: whoever carries it leaves no trace on snow, mud or dust. Poison: no road will keep the one who leaves no trace; every path bends back to the door she fled. The one who sets it loses her own footprints.

**The order:** a merchant's daughter (client by role, no name or title) pays with her pearls; she wants to leave her father's house and leave nothing behind.
- What she made: Madame lifts a sprig from the snow and sets it, pressed, under crystal in a pair of silver shoe buckles. The Silent Buckles. Named only, never a recipe; nothing in them is ever described.
- How it ended: she walked all night and no one could follow; at dawn she stood at her father's gate again, with smooth snow behind her all the way to the horizon.
- The price, seen: Madame walks across the snowy yard and leaves no prints. Now: on the clinic's wet floor the colleague's steps show, hers do not.
- Takes: her footprints.
- Truth of the period (verify before publishing): across the old world, the well-off wore raised wooden pattens over their shoes to keep out of the mud of the streets. Never tied to a country or city.
- The rhyme: in 1551 the rich walked above the mud; now the clinic's floor is kept spotless. No judgement of the procedures.
- Where the daughter wanted to go stays unsaid. No open thread is touched.
