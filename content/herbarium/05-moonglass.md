## Plant card (reference)
MOONGLASS, Imago lunae; folk names: the Pool-Flower, the Widow's Looking-Glass, the Second Face. Grows on the still black water of moorland pools among birches and old wall-stones, only under a young crescent Moon. The reflection in it arrives half a second late. Gift: shows whoever leans over it the face they once loved best on themselves. Poison: it keeps the face it shows; the one who sets it into a glass loses her own reflection.

**The order:** a widow (client by role, no name or title), rich and in mourning, wanted to see herself once more as she was on her wedding night.
- What she made: a petal set into a silver hand-frame, the Pale Glass. Named only, never a recipe.
- How it ended: the widow looked, smiled, and looked again every evening; she stopped leaving the hall. The flower took the heroine's reflection as its price.
- How it answers her: it flattens toward her and darkens like water, and shows another eye next to hers. Whose eye stays an open thread.
- Takes: her reflection.
- Truth of the period (optional, for a later cut; verify before use): in the old world a clear glass mirror was a luxury; most people knew their faces from still water and polished metal. Never tied to a country or city.
- The clinic mirror in 5A is the rhyme: bright mirrors now, black water then. No judgement of the procedures.
